About

I grew up in Cape Town, South Africa, where I began my academic journey studying genetics and computer science. I was always captivated by biology, but increasingly excited by the idea that emerging computational tools could provide new ways to understand it.

The bleeding edge of the genomics industry enticed me to the US, where I completed my Master's in Bioinformatics at Boston University and spent the following years at the Harvard Bioinformatics Core, working on dozens of translational research projects in transcriptomics. Here I got to lead through the full scientific arc of each project — from turning biological questions into computational strategy, through analysis and interpretation, to ultimately communicating results through publication. I gained broad exposure to multiple research areas including juvenile arthritis, aging, lung and liver fibrosis, and leukemia.

The complexity of cancer genomics piqued my interest, and in 2019 I moved to New York City, to join Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center. Here I worked to understand how intensive chemotherapy and radiation in pediatric neuroblastoma patients sometimes leads to therapy related leukemia later in life. This work helped uncover the molecular dynamics of cancer evolution under therapy, and now informs surveillance strategies for pediatric patients at MSK.

In 2022, after my sister experienced the sudden loss of her baby in the NICU, I sought out an opportunity to create impact in this area. I joined the Turro Group at Mount Sinai who were pioneering project NICUnet - a whole genome sequencing initiative identifying rare pathogenic mutations involved in neonatal death.

One of the most rewarding discoveries of my career was identifying a previously unknown mutation that caused a severe form of polycystic kidney disease resulting in the death of a neonate. The finding was experimentally validated and published, and ultimately used to guide IVF for the family's next pregnancy. Their story was covered by TODAY.com.
