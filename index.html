About

I trained as a geneticist and computer scientist in Cape Town, then spent a decade in some of the country's most demanding translational genomics groups — Harvard, Memorial Sloan Kettering, and now Mount Sinai — building the analytical infrastructure that turns sequencing data into decisions clinicians can act on.

My most recent work has shifted toward prediction: building machine learning models on hematologic and genomic data to flag thrombotic risk before it becomes a crisis, and the secure, multi-institution data platforms that make that kind of modeling possible at scale. That combination — messy real-world data, high-stakes predictions, production-grade pipelines — is what I want to bring to an industry ML, data science, or health-data platform team next.

I found a mutation that explained why a newborn had died. It was published, validated in the lab, and used clinically to guide the family's next pregnancy.

Outside of the analysis itself, I've led studies end-to-end across 30+ translational genomics projects, trained graduate researchers, and built course material used to teach RNA-seq analysis at Harvard Medical School. I'm based in New York, NY.
